# Brand Archetypes

Every brand occupies a character role in the customer's mind. Carl Jung's twelve archetypes
name the recurring roles, each mapping to a fundamental human motivation. Use this when the
venture needs a defined brand character — the foundational choice the rest of the brand work
derives from. It answers *who the brand is*, not what it says (`messaging-architecture.md`)
or how it sounds (`tone-of-voice.md`).

## The 12 archetypes

**Motivation: provide structure**

| Archetype | Core motivation | Customer need | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ruler | Control and stability | Feel in charge, organized, successful | Mercedes-Benz, Rolex, Microsoft |
| Caregiver | Service and protection | Feel nurtured, supported, cared for | Johnson & Johnson, UNICEF, Volvo |
| Creator | Innovation and self-expression | Feel creative, original, imaginative | Apple, Lego, Adobe |

**Motivation: pursue independence**

| Archetype | Core motivation | Customer need | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Innocent | Safety and optimism | Feel pure, simple, happy | Coca-Cola, Dove, Whole Foods |
| Explorer | Freedom and discovery | Feel adventurous, pioneering, self-directed | Patagonia, Jeep, Red Bull |
| Sage | Understanding and wisdom | Feel informed, knowledgeable, insightful | Google, BBC, TED |

**Motivation: leave a mark**

| Archetype | Core motivation | Customer need | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Mastery and achievement | Feel courageous, capable, triumphant | Nike, FedEx, BMW |
| Outlaw | Liberation and revolution | Feel rebellious, unconventional, free | Harley-Davidson, Virgin, Diesel |
| Magician | Transformation and power | Feel transformed, empowered, visionary | Disney, Tesla, Dyson |

**Motivation: connect with others**

| Archetype | Core motivation | Customer need | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular Guy | Belonging and authenticity | Feel accepted, down-to-earth, relatable | IKEA, Budweiser, Target |
| Lover | Intimacy and passion | Feel attractive, desired, connected | Chanel, Victoria's Secret, Godiva |
| Jester | Enjoyment and play | Feel entertained, amused, light-hearted | M&M's, Old Spice, Ben & Jerry's |

## Selection principles

1. **Ground in customer evidence.** The archetype must align with the emotional jobs (how
   customers want to feel) and social jobs (how they want to be perceived) already established.
   An aspirational choice with no evidence behind it reads as performative.
2. **One primary, one secondary maximum.** The dominant archetype carries 70–80% of expression;
   at most one secondary adds 20–30% of nuance. Three or more produce incoherence.
3. **Check competitive differentiation.** An archetype that fits the customer but is owned by a
   dominant competitor makes the brand derivative. Audit competitor positioning before settling.
4. **Demand expression, not a label.** An archetype name with no voice, visual, relationship, and
   content-theme definition has no operational value.

## Scoring candidates

Score each archetype 0–3 on three dimensions.

| Dimension | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emotional fit | Directly embodies the primary customer job | Strongly related | Tangentially relevant | Contradicts customer needs |
| Purpose fit | Naturally delivers the value proposition | Strong but not primary | Partial fit | Forced or contradictory |
| Differentiation | No competitor in the category | One partial competitor | Crowded space | A dominant competitor owns it |

## Expression dimensions

For the selected archetype, define all four:

1. **Voice character** — 3–5 adjectives the brand IS, 3–5 it is NOT, plus sample sentences.
2. **Visual style direction** — mood, colors, imagery to embrace and to avoid.
3. **Customer relationship type** — a named dynamic (teacher–student, coach–athlete, guide–explorer).
4. **Content themes** — 4–6 recurring topics, 2–3 topics to avoid.

## Combinations that contradict

- Outlaw + Ruler — rebellion against establishment.
- Innocent + Outlaw — purity against rule-breaking.
- Caregiver + Hero — selfless service against personal triumph (workable with care).
- Sage + Jester — wisdom against irreverence (workable with self-deprecating humor).

Test: can the same brand authentically express both in a single customer interaction?

## What a good output contains

- Emotional territory brief drawn from the customer evidence already gathered.
- Evaluation matrix covering all 12 archetypes with scores.
- Competitor archetype map.
- Primary archetype with justification; secondary archetype or an explicit "none".
- Rejected archetypes with reasons.
- A one-sentence archetype statement.
- The expression guide — all four dimensions.
- **Differentiation stress test, passed**: against each of the top 3 competitors, would a customer
  seeing both side by side feel a distinct character difference within 30 seconds? If no, sharpen
  the failing expression dimension before proceeding.
- **Coherence stress test, passed**: read voice, visuals, relationship and themes together — do
  they belong to the same character, or does one pull away?

## Pitfalls

Aspirational over authentic · personality-quiz thinking (a fast workshop pick with no evidence) ·
blending too many archetypes · skipping the competitor check · a label with no expression.
