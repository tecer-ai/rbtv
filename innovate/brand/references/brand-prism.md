# Brand Prism

Jean-Noël Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism defines a brand's identity through six facets on two
axes: sender (the brand) against receiver (the customer), and externalization (visible to others)
against internalization (felt inside). Use it to turn a chosen archetype into a complete identity
system. It describes *what the brand IS across all six facets*; the competitive claim against
alternatives is `brand-positioning.md`.

## The sequence

Worked in this order, always. Each step consumes the previous one's output, so a skipped or
reordered step is not a shortcut — it silently invalidates everything downstream of it. The sender
facets are built before the receiver facets, and the bridge facet last, because the relationship is
what the two sides have to agree on.

| # | Step | What it produces | What breaks if it is skipped or taken out of order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assemble the prism inputs | The chosen archetype, the customer's social and emotional jobs, and the founder's convictions, each named as the evidence a facet will be built from | Facets get invented rather than derived, and the traceability the output requires cannot be written afterwards |
| 2 | Define physique | 4–6 tangible attributes covering interface aesthetic, interaction patterns, visual signature and product artifacts, plus a first-impression sentence | Physique collapses into "the logo", and the product experience — where customers actually meet the brand — goes undefined |
| 3 | Define personality | 5–7 traits derived from the archetype, each with a behavioral example, plus 3–5 anti-traits | Personality invented fresh contradicts the archetype already chosen, and step 8's pair tests then fail for a reason nobody can fix |
| 4 | Define culture | 3–5 core beliefs in the form "We believe [belief]. That is why we [action]", each failing the copy test — a competitor could not state it without changing their behavior | Generic values enter the prism, and `golden-circle.md`, which takes culture as its strongest Why signal, inherits them |
| 5 | Define reflection | 3–5 statements of "A person who uses [brand] is seen as [perception]", grounded in the customer's social jobs and written about the customer | Written before the sender facets it describes an aspiration with no brand behind it; written about the brand it duplicates personality |
| 6 | Define self-image | 3–5 statements of "When I use [brand], I feel [internal state]", grounded in the customer's emotional jobs | The facet where the deepest loyalty forms is left empty, and the physique has no felt outcome to be checked against |
| 7 | Define the relationship | A named relationship type, what the brand promises and expects in return, situational behaviors at onboarding, success, failure and departure, and a one-paragraph statement | Built before both sides exist, it describes a dynamic with only one participant defined |
| 8 | Test all six pairs | An explicit contradiction assessment across the six listed pairs, with every revision documented | Contradictions remain invisible to the founders and visible to customers, which is precisely how they are found |
| 9 | Read the prism narrative | The single sentence connecting all six facets, read aloud in under 30 seconds | Six individually reasonable facets ship as a character that does not cohere |
| 10 | Write the integration notes | A statement of what each downstream framework receives from this prism | Brandbook, Golden Circle, positioning and tone of voice each re-derive the facets and diverge |

Steps 2, 3 and 4 are sender-side and may be worked in any order among themselves, as may steps 5
and 6 on the receiver side. Nothing else moves.

## The six facets

**Sender facets — the brand side**

| Facet | Definition | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| Physique | Tangible, visible characteristics | What does someone notice on first encounter? |
| Personality | Traits the brand would have as a person | If the brand were a person, how would they act? |
| Culture | Values and beliefs the brand embodies | What worldview makes this brand's approach inevitable? |

**Receiver facets — the customer side**

| Facet | Definition | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| Reflection | How customers appear to others through the brand | What kind of person uses this brand? |
| Self-image | How customers feel internally when using it | How do I feel about myself using this? |

**Bridge facet**

| Facet | Definition | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship | The dynamic between brand and customer | What type of relationship is this? |

## Defining each facet

**Physique.** For a digital product this includes interface aesthetic (clean or dense, minimal or
rich), interaction patterns (fast or deliberate, guided or exploratory), visual signature elements
(color family, typography style, spatial density), and product artifacts (dashboards, reports,
notifications, emails). Format: 4–6 tangible attributes plus a first-impression sentence.

**Personality.** Expands the archetype's voice character into behavior — 5–7 traits, each with one
behavioral example ("Because we are [trait], we [behavior]"), plus 3–5 anti-traits the brand
explicitly is NOT.

**Culture.** Beliefs, not generic values. 3–5 core beliefs that drive existence beyond profit,
connected to the archetype motivation, the value proposition, and founder conviction. Format:
"We believe [belief]. That is why we [action]." Test: if a competitor could copy the statement
without changing their behavior, it is too generic.

**Reflection.** How *others* perceive the customer. 3–5 statements of the form "A person who uses
[brand] is seen as [perception]", grounded in the customer's social jobs. Must be about the
customer, never about the brand.

**Self-image.** How the *customer* feels internally. 3–5 statements of the form "When I use [brand],
I feel [internal state]", grounded in the customer's emotional jobs.

**Relationship.** A named type (mentor–student, coach–athlete, guide–explorer, partner–partner),
what the brand promises and what it expects in return, situational behaviors at onboarding,
success, failure and departure, and a one-paragraph relationship statement.

## Consistency testing

Test each pair for contradiction:

| Pair | Contradiction example |
|---|---|
| Physique ↔ Personality | Cold, corporate visuals with a playful personality |
| Personality ↔ Culture | Rebellious character with compliance-focused values |
| Culture ↔ Relationship | Transparency values, but the relationship withholds information |
| Relationship ↔ Reflection | Protective relationship promising customer independence |
| Reflection ↔ Self-image | "Cutting-edge" appearance but a "confused" feeling |
| Physique ↔ Self-image | Cluttered interface promising a "calm and control" feeling |

## The prism narrative test

One sentence connecting all six:

> "Our brand looks like [physique], acts like [personality], believes [culture], relates to
> customers as [relationship], helps them appear [reflection], and makes them feel [self-image]."

If it reads as a single coherent character, the prism is consistent. It should read aloud in
under 30 seconds.

## What a good output contains

- Prism inputs summary — the evidence each facet was built from.
- All six facets at the depth specified above, each traceable to its evidence.
- Consistency assessment covering all six pairs, with any revisions documented.
- The prism narrative.
- Integration notes stating what each downstream framework receives.

## Pitfalls

Confusing personality (brand character) with reflection (customer appearance) · treating physique
as just a logo instead of the whole product experience · generic culture values ("integrity,
innovation" says nothing) · skipping the consistency test — contradictions stay visible to
customers even when invisible to founders · inventing personality fresh instead of deriving it
from the archetype · ignoring self-image, where the deepest loyalty forms.
