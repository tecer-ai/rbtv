# Golden Circle

Simon Sinek's Golden Circle structures brand identity as three concentric layers: Why (the belief),
How (the differentiated approach), What (the tangible offering). Most organizations communicate
outside-in; the model argues for communicating inside-out. Use it when the venture's purpose is
still implicit and everything downstream needs a fixed point. It answers *why the brand exists*;
the place it occupies against alternatives is `brand-positioning.md`.

## The sequence

Worked in this order, always. Each step consumes the previous one's output, so a skipped or
reordered step is not a shortcut — it silently invalidates everything downstream of it. The circle
is filled inside-out, Why first, and that direction is the whole argument of the model.

| # | Step | What it produces | What breaks if it is skipped or taken out of order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gather the purpose signals | The Why's raw material pulled from the prism's culture facet, the customer's emotional jobs, the founder narrative, the founder's operating beliefs and the archetype's motivation — with each source named | The Why gets invented in the room, and the endurance and authenticity tests at step 5 then have nothing to test against |
| 2 | Draft the Why with the founder present | One sentence — "We believe that [statement about the world]" — plus a supporting narrative tracing it to the signals | A Why written for the founder rather than by them fails the authenticity test permanently; it cannot be delegated |
| 3 | Derive the How | 2–3 principles in the form "We [action] because we believe [connection to the Why]", each observable and each tied to a specific line of the Why | Skipped, the Why has no operational consequence and lives only on the About page — the model's most common failure |
| 4 | State the What | 1–2 plain sentences naming a recognizable category in customer language, with no superlatives and no purpose language | Written first, the What becomes the thing the Why is reverse-engineered to justify, which produces "we exist to build the best platform" |
| 5 | Run the chain aloud | "We believe [Why]. That is why we [How]. And that is how we deliver [What]." spoken end to end, with the weaker element revised wherever a link feels forced | Three separately plausible statements ship as a purpose that does not actually connect |
| 6 | Run the three validation tests | Endurance, authenticity and motivation, each explicitly passed | A product-specific or performative Why is carried into positioning, messaging and tone, where it contaminates all three |
| 7 | Check against the prism and the archetype | A written consistency check, with any contradiction resolved rather than noted | The brand ends up with two identities that disagree, and each downstream framework picks whichever it read last |

Nothing here may be worked out of order — in particular, the What is never stated before the Why
and the How exist.

## The three layers

### Why — purpose, cause, belief

The belief that drives the organization beyond profit.

Questions: What do you believe about the world? Why does this company exist beyond making money?
What would be lost if you stopped existing?

Criteria — the Why:
- does NOT mention product, category, or features;
- would survive a complete product pivot;
- connects to a customer emotional job;
- the founder can say "I believe this" without hesitation;
- is specific enough that competitors would not use the same words.

Format: "We believe that [statement about the world]."

### How — the differentiated approach

The approach, values, or process that distinguishes execution.

Questions: How does your belief change how you build? What do you do differently because of what
you believe? What observable behaviors distinguish you?

Criteria: each principle connects explicitly to the Why, is observable by customers or team
members, reflects the venture's unfair advantage, and is distinct from competitor approaches.

Format: 2–3 principles — "We [action] because we believe [connection to the Why]."

### What — the tangible offering

Questions: What do you actually make or sell? How would a customer describe it? What category do
you operate in?

Criteria: names a recognizable product category, uses customer language rather than internal
terminology, stays plain and specific with no superlatives or purpose language, and connects to
How and Why through the narrative chain.

Format: 1–2 sentences.

## The chain

> "We believe [Why]. That is why we [How]. And that is how we deliver [What]."

Read it aloud. If any link feels forced, the weaker element needs revision.

## The three validation tests

| Test | The question | Failure means |
|---|---|---|
| Endurance | "Pivot to a completely different product in the same domain — does the Why still hold?" | The Why is too product-specific |
| Authenticity | "Can the founder say this at a dinner table without feeling performative?" | The Why does not reflect genuine conviction |
| Motivation | "Does this make a potential team member or customer want to be part of this?" | The Why lacks emotional charge |

All three must pass before the framework is complete. A founder cannot delegate the Why — external
parties cannot invent it.

## Where the Why comes from

| Signal source | What to extract |
|---|---|
| Brand prism culture facet | Core beliefs and values |
| Customer emotional jobs | What customers need to feel |
| Founder narrative | The "why it matters" behind the venture |
| Founder operating beliefs | Tenets the founder already works by |
| Archetype motivation | The universal drive behind the chosen archetype |

Common Why patterns, each with its trap: injustice correction ("no one should have to…" — do not
exaggerate the injustice) · democratization (be specific about what and for whom) · transformation
(connect it to a real customer desire) · challenging the status quo (name what is actually broken)
· possibility creation (make the outcome concrete).

## What a good output contains

- Purpose signals summary — the sources that informed the Why.
- Why statement (one sentence) plus a supporting narrative with traceability.
- 2–3 How principles, each with an observable example.
- A plain What statement.
- The complete Why-How-What chain.
- Results of all three validation tests.
- Consistency check against the prism and the archetype, with contradictions resolved.

## Pitfalls

Confusing Why with What ("we exist to build the best platform" is a What) · generic purpose ("we
believe in empowering people" is true of every company) · skipping How, which leaves the Why with
no operational impact · treating it as a marketing exercise — a Why that only appears on the About
page failed · defining it without the founder.
