# Brand Positioning

Positioning defines the specific place a brand occupies in the customer's mind relative to the
alternatives they would otherwise use. Use it once purpose and identity exist and the venture needs
a competitive claim. The positioning statement is an **internal strategic document**, not a tagline
and not customer-facing copy — turning it into copy is `messaging-architecture.md`.

## The positioning statement

> "For **[target segment]** who **[need/job]**, **[brand]** is the **[category]** that
> **[key benefit]**, unlike **[primary alternative]** which **[key limitation]**."

| Element | What it is | What it is NOT |
|---|---|---|
| Target segment | A specific person or organization in a specific situation | A broad demographic label |
| Need / job | A functional or emotional job in the customer's own language | A product-feature need |
| Category | A recognizable category customers already budget for | A made-up jargon category |
| Key benefit | The single most important outcome — purpose made tangible | A feature list |
| Primary alternative | What the target most likely uses today | The weakest competitor |
| Key limitation | A real, acknowledged shortcoming of that alternative | A straw-man weakness |

## The perceptual map

A two-dimensional plot of competitors along strategically relevant axes.

| Axis 1 | Axis 2 | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | Powerful | Productivity / software tools |
| Self-serve | Full-service | Service businesses |
| Affordable | Premium | Consumer products |
| Generalist | Specialist | Professional services |
| Automated | Manual | Process tools |
| Individual | Team | Collaboration software |

Construction: choose two dimensions the target actually evaluates alternatives on → plot 5–8
competitors including the "do nothing" option → plot the intended position → identify white space →
verify the white space carries a real customer need. Empty is not automatically valuable.

## The four validation tests

Every statement must pass all four.

| Test | The question | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Uniqueness | Could any competitor credibly make the same claim? | Revise the benefit, the alternative, or the category frame |
| Credibility | What evidence supports the benefit claim? | List capabilities, testimonials, architecture, expertise; if aspirational, document a gap-closing plan |
| Relevance | Does the benefit directly address the customer's primary job? | The benefit is irrelevant — return to the job evidence |
| Consistency | Does it align with the purpose, the identity facets, and the business model? | Document and resolve the contradiction |

## Gathering the inputs

| Section | Content | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Who we position for (segment, role, situation) | Business model and customer narrative |
| Need | What job or problem we address | Customer jobs evidence |
| Benefit | What we promise — purpose made tangible | `golden-circle.md` Why + How |
| Category | What kind of thing we are | `golden-circle.md` What |
| Alternatives | Who we position against | The venture's competitive landscape work |
| Differentiators | Why we are different | How principles, unfair advantage, `brand-prism.md` |

## What a good output contains

- The positioning inputs brief, all six sections.
- Draft variations with annotations of what each traded off.
- The final positioning statement with per-element annotation.
- The perceptual map: axes, competitors, brand position, white space.
- Results of all four validation tests with the evidence behind each.
- Integration notes naming what downstream work receives.

## Pitfalls

1. **Feature-based positioning** — "the only platform with real-time sync and 50+ integrations" is
   fragile; competitors copy features. Write the benefit as a customer outcome.
2. **An unrecognizable category** — inventing a category forces market education before selling.
   Start from the category the target already searches for, then add a modifier.
3. **A straw-man alternative** — picking a weak competitor with an obvious flaw reads as dishonest.
   Choose the strongest relevant alternative with a real, hard-to-fix limitation.
4. **Skipping the perceptual map** — writing the statement without visualizing the space risks
   invisible overlap. Build the map first.
5. **Publishing positioning as messaging** — it is internal strategy. Translate it through
   `messaging-architecture.md` before it reaches a customer.

## Builds on / feeds

Owns the **positioning statement and perceptual map**. Builds on `golden-circle.md` (Why → benefit,
How → differentiation, What → category), `brand-prism.md` (physique and personality shape the
competitive frame), and the venture's business-model and market-sizing work.
`messaging-architecture.md` references it for message differentiation.
