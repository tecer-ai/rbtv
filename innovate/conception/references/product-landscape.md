# Product landscape

The product landscape is the founder's product vision written down as modules and features.
It is built with the benchmark synthesis in view but is NOT a summary of it: the benchmarks
supply context, the founder supplies the vision. Use it after `benchmark-analysis.md`, once
a comparative synthesis exists, to convert competitive evidence into a product the founder
can defend module by module.

## Vision before structure

Three questions come first, one at a time, each answered before the next is asked. Nothing is
proposed until all three are answered.

1. "Forget the benchmarks for a moment. In your own words: what does your product DO? What is
   the core action?"
2. "Who is the primary user? What is their job title, their daily pain?"
3. "What is the single output that makes the user say 'this was worth it'?"

## Module discovery

Propose an initial module list from those answers — not from the benchmark taxonomy. The
founder's product may organize entirely differently from the products it was benchmarked
against, and copying the taxonomy across is the failure mode this step exists to prevent.

For EVERY proposed module, three questions: why does this module exist? Without it, does the
product still deliver value? Is it core to the value proposition, an enabler, or a future
differentiator? The founder adds, removes, renames, and reclassifies until the list is
stable.

## Feature mapping

Per approved module, work three lists:

- Which features from the benchmarks are relevant here — read from the synthesis, never from
  the raw benchmark documents.
- Which features the founder wants that NO benchmark has.
- Which benchmark features the founder explicitly does NOT want, and why. These become the
  **conscious omissions** — recorded with their rationale, because an omission that is not
  written down gets rediscovered and re-argued later, and because a deliberate omission is a
  positioning decision.

## Strategic classification

Every feature gets exactly one:

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| V1 core | Must exist in the first version |
| V1 optional | Wanted in V1, can be cut under pressure |
| Later | Planned, not V1 |
| Maybe never | Captured for completeness, unlikely to be built |

## The standing challenge

Throughout, one question is asked of anything that appears: **"Is this here because the
benchmarks have it, or because your user needs it?"** Push back whenever the founder is
copying instead of reasoning from first principles. This step is the most interactive of the
conception frameworks — provoke, challenge, surface the trade-offs. The output is a decision
record, not a feature inventory.

## What a good output contains

- A product vision summary in the founder's own words, from the three opening questions.
- A module map where every module carries the stated reason it exists.
- A feature matrix in which every feature carries exactly one strategic classification.
- Conscious omissions: the benchmark features explicitly rejected, each with its rationale.
- The open questions that remain — named, not quietly dropped.
