# Working Backwards

Working Backwards writes the product's launch announcement first, in customer language,
before anything is built. The press release forces a clear customer, a clear problem, and a
benefit stated as a change in the customer's world. The FAQs then force everything the press
release glosses over into the open. Use it as the FIRST conception framework: it originates
the customer definition, the problem statement, and the value proposition that every later
framework refines.

## The structure

**Press release — one page, ten parts.**

| # | Part | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Headline | The core customer benefit — never the technology |
| 2 | Subheading | The primary customer, and what changes for them |
| 3 | Dateline | Location and the future launch date |
| 4 | Intro paragraph | Context and category |
| 5 | Problem paragraph | The customer's pain, described vividly and concretely |
| 6 | Solution paragraph | How the product removes that pain |
| 7 | Leader quote | Why this matters |
| 8 | How it works | The typical customer journey |
| 9 | Customer quote | Hypothetical, from an archetypal customer |
| 10 | Getting started | Access, pricing, onboarding |

**External FAQ** — what customers, partners, and stakeholders would ask. Themes: what the
product is and how it works; eligibility and fit; pricing and commercial terms; integration
and migration; data, privacy, and trust; support and reliability.

**Internal FAQ** — the uncomfortable questions, ending in a written answer to *is it worth
doing?* Themes: feasibility and resources (team, technology, time); economics (market size,
unit economics, pricing logic); dependencies and risks (partners, regulation, data, platform);
strategic fit; success metrics and kill criteria.

## Questions that produce the substance

For the customer and problem brief: who is the primary customer (role, organization size,
industry)? What job are they trying to get done? What are their current workarounds? What
frictions hurt most?

For the internal FAQ: what capabilities must be built that do not exist yet? What are the top
technical unknowns? What must be true about customer behavior for the economics to work?
What would make this project worth killing after three to six months?

## What a good output contains

- A customer and problem brief naming at least three real organizations or people who fit,
  with a problem statement that makes sense without mentioning the product, that a third
  party can paraphrase after one read, and that lists at least two current alternatives.
- A press release that fits one page, states customer and problem in its first two
  paragraphs, carries no internal jargon, and reads as compelling to an outsider.
- An external FAQ with at least ten substantial questions answered concretely, where every
  promise the press release makes has a question clarifying its limits, and every assumption
  is tagged for later validation.
- An internal FAQ with a written answer to "is it worth doing?", the top risks and
  assumptions listed, and no hidden dependencies.
- A synthesis of roughly 250 words: primary customer, core problem, solution value, the
  decision, and the top three assumptions to validate.

## Pitfalls

Writing for stakeholders instead of customers. Treating the PR/FAQ as marketing copy instead
of writing the awkward questions into the internal FAQ. Calling the press release done
without the internal FAQ. Over-specifying implementation instead of locking what must be true
for the customer. Never killing an idea — define kill criteria in advance, and record a kill
as the method working.

## Builds on / feeds

Builds on nothing — it is the first framework of the trail and the originator of the customer
definition, the problem statement, and the value proposition. Problem-Solution Fit refines the
segment boundaries and adds triggers and emotional dimensions; Lean Canvas maps segments to
channels, distills the problem to a top-three list and the value proposition to a single UVP
statement; Five Whys traces the problem to its root causes; the internal FAQ's assumptions
seed the validation milestone's Leap of Faith inventory.
