# Leap of Faith

Every new venture rests on beliefs nobody has tested. Leap of Faith turns that diffuse cloud into an
explicit ranked inventory, and that inventory sets the agenda for the whole validation milestone.
Use it FIRST in validation, once the concept exists. The output is not a plan to be right; it is a
plan to learn fast and fail cheaply.

## The structure

**1. Harvest.** Extract assumptions from every conception artifact — the explicit ones and, more
importantly, the implicit ones nobody wrote down. Expect 15–30+ traceable assumptions.

**2. Classify.** Every assumption is a Value hypothesis or a Growth hypothesis, with a sub-category.

| Value hypothesis — sub-category | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Problem existence | Does this pain actually exist? |
| Problem severity | Is it painful enough to motivate action? |
| Solution fit | Does our solution address the pain? |
| Willingness to switch | Will they leave their current workaround? |
| Retention & habit | Will they keep using it? |

| Growth hypothesis — sub-category | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Channel effectiveness | Can we reach customers through this channel? |
| Acquisition cost | Can we afford to acquire them this way? |
| Conversion rate | Will prospects convert at this rate? |
| Pricing & willingness to pay | Will they pay this price? |
| Revenue model | Will this revenue structure work? |
| Retention economics | Will renewals sustain the business? |
| Referral & virality | Will customers refer others? |
| Market size | Is the market large enough? |

**3. Rank.** Score each assumption on two 1–5 scales; priority = impact × uncertainty.

| Score | Impact — if wrong | Uncertainty — evidence held |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | No viable business exists | Pure speculation, founder intuition only |
| 4 | Major pivot required | Indirect or analogical evidence only |
| 3 | Significant redesign needed | Some evidence, but not from the target segment |
| 2 | Moderate adjustment required | Direct evidence, limited sample |
| 1 | Minor parameter change | Well established, multiple data points |

High impact × high uncertainty = the true leap-of-faith assumptions. Take the top 5–10.

**4. Define signals.** For each top assumption: invalidation signal, validation signal, signal
threshold, collection method, timeline, owner. Write the INVALIDATION signal first — defining what
would prove the belief wrong before defining what would confirm it is what keeps confirmation bias
out of the design.

**5. Pre-commit decision rules.** Kill: what combination of invalidated assumptions stops the
venture? Pivot: what evidence distinguishes "wrong solution" from "wrong problem"? Persevere: what
POSITIVE evidence is required — not merely the absence of bad news? Add aggregate rules (how many
Value assumptions may fail, how many Growth), a maximum time and money budget before the go/no-go,
and a review schedule naming who decides and when.

**6. Validation backlog.** Map each top assumption to the specific later activity that will test it,
with a one-sentence directive to that activity ("market sizing must show an addressable market above
X for segment Y; return the estimate with a confidence range").

## What a good output contains

- A consolidated inventory of 15–30+ assumptions, each traceable to where it came from.
- Every assumption classified Value or Growth WITH its sub-category, and assumptions present in both
  categories — an all-Value or all-Growth inventory means the harvest was partial.
- An impact × uncertainty score for every assumption, and the top 5–10 stated as explicit sentences.
- A signal spec per top assumption, invalidation signal first.
- Kill, pivot and persevere criteria that are REACHABLE inside the validation timeline. A criterion
  that can never trigger is theatre, not a criterion. "If zero customers want it" is a cop-out.
- A validation backlog in which every top assumption maps to at least one downstream activity.

Failure looks like: only the explicit assumptions harvested, low uncertainty scores assigned to
avoid discomfort, unfalsifiable kill criteria, and a backlog frozen after one pass. The inventory is
live — update it after every validation activity produces evidence.

## Builds on / feeds

Builds on every conception framework (working backwards, jobs-to-be-done, competitive landscape,
problem-solution fit, lean canvas, five whys) — it harvests from their outputs. It OWNS the
assumption inventory and its Value/Growth classification: `assumption-mapping.md` scores and
prioritizes that inventory rather than rebuilding it, `tam-sam-som.md` and `unit-economics.md` and
`technology-readiness-level.md` each take the assumptions flagged for them, and `pre-mortem.md`
aligns its failure modes to these kill criteria.
