# narrative-quality — data integrity, source discipline, message-lock, audience-side challenge

Narrative-quality judgements. NEVER visual ones. This reference NEVER carries palette, typeface, font, grid, motif, layout, colour, chart style, component choices, HTML, hex values, or assigned slide numbers. Those are out of scope — refuse them.

Reached through the capability that needs it (`narrative-lock`), not on its own.

---

## Data integrity

**D-1** Use ONLY titles present in source documents — NEVER infer CEO, COO, CTO, or any other title. If titles are ambiguous or missing, ask the owner.

**D-2** Use full legal/professional names in investor-facing pieces. NEVER use nicknames or informal names. If source documents use nicknames, resolve from CVs or ask the owner.

**D-3** NEVER include a named fund or investor-partner quote in an outbound investor piece (relationship, fundraising, or update). Existing investors seeing another investor's endorsement creates social dynamics the founder MUST control.

**D-4** If a stat's source category does not match the company's positioning, NEVER rename the market category to fit. Flag the tension to the owner and present options (keep with honest label, remove, or find alternative data). Misattributed data destroys credibility if the audience checks the source.

**D-5** SAM and TAM MUST include full names ("Serviceable Addressable Market," "Total Addressable Market") alongside abbreviations on first use. Every market number MUST be self-explanatory at a glance — no unexplained figures.

---

## Source research

**SR-1** The narrative document is NOT sufficient source material for team content. ALWAYS read actual CV/bio files for every founder before generating team content. If CVs are unavailable, ask the owner before generating.

**SR-2** Every founder profile MUST show the same number and depth of career highlights. If one founder has 3 highlights with company names, all MUST have 3 highlights with company names. If source material for one founder is thin, ask the owner for more detail — NEVER generate a shallow profile.

## Message lock

**ML-1** Narrative First is the foundational ordering rule: the story is agreed before ANY visual work. Story drives what is seen, not the other way around. This is non-negotiable — generating visuals before narrative lock is a failure condition.

**ML-2** The handoff is a file-based contract readable with zero conversation context: `narrative-lock.md` (the narrative role's output) plus `visual-communication-plan.md` (the visual strategist's). Downstream work reads them from disk. NEVER hand off through conversation memory.

**ML-3** `narrative-lock.md` and `visual-communication-plan.md` are a linked unit. When the story changes, update BOTH in the same operation. NEVER let the visual-communication-plan go stale against the lock.

**ML-4** Titles are the POINT, not a label. "Traction" fails. "$120K MRR, 40% MoM" passes. Every title MUST state the takeaway — what the audience should think after reading it.

**ML-5** Data discussion is conceptual before research: identify WHAT data would validate each beat and WHERE it might exist — do NOT present specific numbers unless they already exist in the brief. Discuss data types and sources, not specific values. The goal is a data wishlist the research brief pursues.

**ML-6** Research asks MUST be broader than specific data points (room for adjacent or proxy data). NEVER send a shopping list of exact numbers — the executor does not hold the narrative.

**ML-7** Read source documents before drafting. Do NOT re-ask the owner questions the documents already answer. If CV/bio files exist, read them before drafting team content.

## Client / buyer mode

**SC-1** Think in BUSINESS OUTCOMES, not features. Every beat is evaluated against "Would this survive a procurement committee review?" The kill question for every beat: "Would I sign a contract based on this?"

**SC-2** Five client principles to enforce at every challenge: (1) "I don't buy products — I buy solutions to problems I can quantify." (2) "Every vendor says they're different. Show me the proof, not the claim." (3) "If you can't show ROI early, I'm checking email before the proof arrives." (4) "The best client pitches answer my objections before I raise them." (5) "I need to defend this purchase to my CFO and my board. Make it easy for me."

**SC-3** No kitchen details: NEVER expose internal implementation details in a client pitch — technology stack, AI model choices, infrastructure decisions, internal tooling, or engineering architecture. Describe capabilities and outcomes, never the machinery behind them.

**SC-4** Positive scope by default: frame scope by describing what the solution IS and what it delivers. NEVER default to negative scope statements ("we will NOT do X") — they invite "why not?" and plant doubt. If negative scope exists in the context, capture it internally and ask the owner whether to state it explicitly.

**SC-5** Frame EVERYTHING from the CLIENT'S perspective, never the vendor's. "You'll gain X" not "We're the best at X." Feature dumps instead of outcome-focused messaging = failure condition. The artifact language MUST match the client's language, not the vendor's internal language.

**SC-7** Common buyer objections to research and pre-empt: real total cost of ownership (hidden costs, integration, training); who else in this industry uses this (references, case studies); what happens if this doesn't work (SLAs, exit clauses); how this compares to specific competitors; implementation risk (timeline, resources, disruption).

## Investor mode

**SI-1** Sit ACROSS the table, not beside it. Every question is the question VCs ask in partner meetings WHEN THE FOUNDER LEAVES THE ROOM. Zero sycophancy. When a narrative is weak, say "I wouldn't take this to my partners — here's why." When it's strong, say "This works. Move on."

**SI-2** Five investor principles to enforce at every challenge: (1) "I don't invest in slides — I invest in founders who understand their business cold." (2) "The best pitch decks answer questions before I ask them." (3) "If you can't explain your defensibility in one sentence, you don't have any." (4) "Show me the smallest possible market where you win — then show me how it expands." (5) "Every number in your deck is a promise. Don't put numbers you can't defend."

**SI-3** For every investor beat, challenge it with the hard question a VC would ask, then assess whether the current narrative answers it. If not, state what is missing or weak AND propose a concrete alternative. NEVER rubber-stamp. NEVER present a challenge without a concrete alternative or better angle.

**SI-4** Narrative assessment at the end of the lock: Story arc strength (Strong / Needs work / Weak + why) → beats that land → beats that need work (with specific problems) → missing from the story → Kill question (the single hardest question; does the lock answer it?).

**SI-5** Counter-thesis research is mandatory: generate a genuinely adversarial research prompt structured as a VC analyst finding reasons NOT to invest. Research: key business model risks, market headwinds, competitive threats (larger players, well-funded competitors), regulatory risks, technology risks, historical failures in this space and why they failed.
